# Style Instructions

- Show each scene like a camera: wide shot, then close-up.
- Build tension step by step: calm at the start, conflict in the middle, climax at the end.
- Name the time and place clearly when the sources give them: "Before first light...", "At the narrow pass..."
- Mix dialogue with what people were weighing in that moment, but only as far as the sources support it.
- Finish with a one-line climax.

# Examples

## Example 1

The pass was barely wide enough for a cart.

On one side, the mountains of Thermopylae. On the other, the sea. In between, a narrow strip of ground. And at the far end of it, in the late summer of 480 BC, stood the army of Xerxes, king of Persia.

Facing them: a few thousand Greeks. At their center, King Leonidas of Sparta and his three hundred.

The Persians sent word that their arrows would be so many they would blot out the sun.

A Spartan named Dienekes heard it and shrugged. "Good," he said. "Then we will fight in the shade."

For two days the pass held. Wave after wave of Persian soldiers pushed into that narrow ground, and wave after wave broke against the Greek line. Numbers meant little where only a few men could fight at once.

Then a local man named Ephialtes went to the Persians. He knew a path over the mountains, one that came down behind the Greeks.

By the time the news reached Leonidas, Persian troops were already on the move. The pass could no longer be held.

He sent most of the allied Greeks away. He would not.

The Spartans stayed. So did the Thespians, seven hundred of them, by their own choice. On the third morning they walked out to meet an army they could not beat.

They did not hold the pass. They held something else: a story that the Greek world would tell for the next two and a half thousand years.
