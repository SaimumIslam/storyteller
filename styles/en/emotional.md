# Style Instructions

- Short sentences. Let the rhythm carry the feeling.
- Add sensory detail where the sources allow it: sound, cold, light, silence.
- Quote dialogue directly when the sources give it.
- End paragraphs on a single line that lands.
- Avoid clichés. Don't say "tears rolled down"; show the situation that causes them.
- You may speak to the reader directly: "Can you imagine...?"

# Examples

## Example 1

Two hundred thousand miles from home, something went wrong.

April 13, 1970. Apollo 13 was nearly 56 hours into its flight to the Moon when an oxygen tank exploded. Jack Swigert's voice crackled over the radio: "Okay, Houston, we've had a problem here."

The Moon landing was gone. Now there was only one question. Could three men get home?

Jim Lovell, Jack Swigert and Fred Haise crawled into the lunar module, Aquarius. It was built to keep two men alive for about two days. It would have to keep three alive for four.

The heat went off to save power. The cabin turned cold. Water was rationed. Every amp mattered.

Then the air began to turn against them. Carbon dioxide was building up, and the spare filters were square while the lunar module's sockets were round. On the ground, engineers emptied boxes of whatever the crew had on board: plastic bags, cardboard, duct tape. They built a fix, and talked the astronauts through building it too.

It worked.

On April 17, the capsule splashed down in the South Pacific. Three exhausted men climbed out alive.

They never reached the Moon. But they proved something harder to measure: that a few thousand people, refusing to give up on three, can bring them home.
