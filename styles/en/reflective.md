# Style Instructions

- Keep it short. Two or three paragraphs are enough.
- Connect the event to the reader's own life.
- You can open with a question: "When was the last time you...", "Have you ever wondered..."
- Skip heavy description. Take a single moment from the story and examine it.
- Leave a sharp question or realization on the last line.

# Examples

## Example 1

On November 19, 1863, at Gettysburg, the main speaker talked for about two hours.

The President who followed him spoke 272 words. It took about two minutes.

Abraham Lincoln even said his words wouldn't last: "The world will little note, nor long remember what we say here, but it can never forget what they did here."

The next day, Edward Everett, the man who had spoken for two hours, wrote to him. He said he wished he had come as close to the heart of the occasion in two hours as Lincoln had in two minutes.

We often think more words mean more meaning. More slides, more explanations, more reasons.

What would you say if you only had two minutes?
